## Provenance

BodyParts3D → Z-Anatomy → Anatria3D GLB adaptation.

The upstream GLB pipeline documents baked Blender modifiers, Draco position/normal quantisation, viewer-side material replacement, stable organ identifiers, and hierarchy export. It states that no polygon reduction is applied beyond Draco quantisation.

## Connective-tissue rendering

The muscular asset includes thin connective structures such as fascia, tendons, aponeuroses, retinacula, ligaments, and intermuscular septa. Many of these sheets lie directly against muscle geometry.

That creates two separate visualization problems:

1. A broad fascial sheet drawn as an opaque solid can visually hide the muscle it wraps.
2. Nearly coplanar fascia and muscle surfaces can compete in the depth buffer and produce z-fighting / blotchy boundaries.

v4 addresses those rendering problems with subtype-specific opacity and a small polygon depth bias. These are rendering treatments only; they do not move the measured anatomy in world space and do not create missing geometry.

## Nomenclature

The manifest exposes Terminologia Anatomica 2 Latin and clinical English. These professional labels are used directly by the interface.

## Scope and validation

The geometry is not generated by AI. The site preserves source structure identifiers and does not invent missing structures.

Automated tests cover viewer behavior and rendering rules, not independent anatomical validation. Anatomical completeness and geometric accuracy inherit the strengths and limitations of the upstream open datasets. A commercial closed atlas may include manually curated structures, continuity, attachments, textures, metadata, or mesh corrections that are not present here.

The site must therefore not be described as a validated diagnostic, radiology, or surgical-navigation system.
